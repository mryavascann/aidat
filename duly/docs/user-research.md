# Early user research

**Survey date:** 19 September 2026, as written on the supplied notes.  
**Sample:** nine respondent blocks in a founder-supplied handwritten survey.  
**Purpose:** explore visibility of building funds, past disputes and interest in a shared treasury.

## Results

| Question | Yes | No | Unclear or absent | Total respondents |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Can you see your building's treasury balance? | 0 | 9 | 0 | 9 |
| Was there a dues or spending disagreement in the past year? | 5 | 3 | 1 | 9 |
| Would you use the proposed shared treasury? | 7 | 2 | 0 | 9 |

The strongest finding is **all 9 respondents reporting no visibility into the building balance**. Seven expressed interest in using the product and two declined. Counts are shown directly because this is a small sample. Unclear or missing answers remain in their own category, rather than becoming a yes or a no. Willingness to use the product is a statement of intent, not a record of activation or usage.

## What people were asked

The supplied question reference covers:

1. Whether residents can see the building's treasury balance.
2. Whether dues or spending caused a disagreement in the past year.
3. Who manages the money, such as a neighbor or a management company.
4. Whether they would switch to a shared treasury where everyone can see funds and members approve spending.
5. What would prevent them from switching.

The survey owner clarified that the answer sheet labels names as item 1, then uses items 2-6 for these five questions. Some blocks skip a question or shift that numbering. The owner subsequently clarified the affected usage-intent answers and corrected one balance-visibility answer from yes to no. The table includes those direct corrections. One dispute answer remains unclear or absent and is excluded from the yes/no counts.

## What we learned

Balance visibility is a concrete problem to test in a pilot. The open-ended notes also mention regulation, manager support and adaptation. These themes inform follow-up conversations, but their frequency is not ranked because several notes are incomplete or hard to interpret.

The next test is to observe whether residents and a manager use the shared balance and spending workflow in a real building. Manager onboarding, repeated use and willingness to pay need separate validation. A reporting or multi-building subscription remains a business hypothesis.

## Limits of the evidence

- The sample is small and informal. Participant recruitment, apartment counts and respondent roles were not systematically recorded.
- The usage question describes the desired benefits, which may encourage a favorable response.
- The notes do not establish that respondents tried the demo, became users, agreed to a pilot or would pay for the product. Check marks beside names are not treated as proof of any of those actions.
- Names and the raw page are omitted from the public repository. This page publishes only aggregate results. No attributed quotes are used.
- This is early discovery evidence, not a representative population estimate or proof of product-market fit.

**Source:** the survey owner's supplied question reference, handwritten notes, explanation of the answer numbering and subsequent answer corrections. The aggregate table is a transcription of that evidence, not an independent audit of the interviews.
